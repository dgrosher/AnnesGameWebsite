# Anne’s Arcade

A cheerful, responsive collection of browser games made for kids and their grown-ups.

## Run locally

Requires Python 3 to serve the site. No dependencies or build step are needed.

```sh
npm start
```

Open port 3000 in your browser. Alternatively run `python3 -m http.server 3000` from this directory.

## Games

- **Memory Match:** choose 4×4 (8 pairs), 6×6 (18 pairs), 8×8 (32 pairs), or 10×10 (50 pairs). Mismatch reveals last a second; enlarge/scroll the board for larger touch targets. Changing size resets the round.
- **Snake:** arrow keys, WASD, or touch buttons to steer. Start/pause with the button; Space also pauses when focus is outside a button. Switching tabs pauses the game.

- **UNO:** play against the computer with action cards, wild colors, and an UNO call button. Includes in-game rules for this casual two-player version.
- **Set Match:** find sets across four features, with hints and additional deals.
- **The Secret Garden:** an original choose-your-own-adventure with four endings.
- **Anne’s Game:** an original number-matching game against the computer. Four each of numbers 1–6, six Draw 2 wilds, and five cards per player. Match the previous number or play a wild. If unable to play, draw one and end your turn; the next player may play any card. Draw 2 makes the opponent draw two and miss their turn, then opens play to any card. First empty hand wins.

- **Finn’s Treehouse:** a 14-page illustrated read-aloud story and firefly-finding game. Each page has one sentence and its own picture. Read one page or the whole story with automatic page turns. The narrator automatically prefers natural, neural, or enhanced English voices when available; choose a storyteller and Gentle, Natural, or Lively pacing. Voice and pace preferences are saved on your device. Some voices require internet; if a selected voice fails or never starts, narration retries an available device voice and then the browser default. Speech resumes a paused browser engine, retains the utterance during playback, and reports startup failures. Browser text-to-speech availability depends on the device and installed voices; reading and the picture game work without audio. No microphone is used.

- **Space Invaders:** move with Left/Right or A/D and fire with Space. Hold the on-screen buttons on touch devices. Clear waves, score points, and protect your three lives.
- **Pong:** play against the computer; first to seven wins. Move with Up/Down or W/S, hold touch buttons, or drag on the court. Pong’s Up/Down touch buttons are stacked vertically. Both arcade games support P to pause and pause automatically when you switch tabs.

- **Fox & Found:** an original cooperative deduction game for one to four detectives sharing one screen, inspired by cooperative fox mysteries. Choose clues or suspects, roll up to three times with matching dice automatically kept, an animated roll, and a 2.6-second result pause before the outcome (the dice stay visible afterward), and identify the pie thief before eight escape steps. Every mystery mixes “wears” and “does not wear” clues for hats, scarves, and glasses. Three or four clues uniquely identify the thief; when scarf clues are negative, both incorrect colors can be ruled out. All players win or lose together; accusations require confirmation. No online multiplayer or accounts.

- **Cloudwing Couriers:** an original flying arcade adventure inspired by aerial jousting games. Pip rides a moonwing moth, meets cloud sprites from above, and collects the letters they release. Move with Left/Right or A/D; hold Space to flap. Three hearts, cloud platforms, and increasingly busy delivery routes.
- **Root & Rumble:** an original digging arcade adventure. Mina Mole digs tunnels and sprinkles grumblebugs into flowers. Move with arrows/WASD; face a bug and tap or hold Space for three dew bursts within three dug squares. Watering progress never fades, and three dots show the hits on each bug. Completed gardens stay visible until you choose Next garden. Three hearts and progressively busier gardens. Both games have original canvas artwork, holdable touch controls, P to pause, and automatic pause when leaving the tab.

- **Ami’s Garden Jigsaw:** exactly 50 interlocking pieces (10 × 5) of an original illustrated garden. Drag pieces or select a piece and tap its board square. Includes a picture preview, edge filter, paged tray, larger scrollable board, keyboard placement, and completion celebration. No timer or rotation.

- **Ami’s Animal Go Fish:** a learning game against the computer with 52 illustrated cards: two of each of 26 A–Z animals. Choose a card to ask for its match, draw when told to go fish, and collect two-card pairs in the alphabet album. Seven-card opening hands; empty hands refill up to five while the pond has cards. Matching requests or fishing the requested card earn another turn. Most pairs wins after all 26 are found. Computer actions hold for three seconds, then display the requested animal for 3.5 seconds before moving cards. New pairs get a 2.6-second animated celebration; a Pause computer button lets players stop and read. Uppercase/lowercase letters, animal names, an explorable album, and optional browser narration support learning.

- **Sunseed Rally:** an original garden kart race against three computer rivals. Choose Finn, Ruby, Mina, or Bea; complete three laps. Up/W accelerates, Left/Right or A/D changes lanes, Space boosts, Down/S brakes. The kart follows the bends automatically. Collect seeds to recharge boost; dodge mud and rival karts. Includes a countdown, live placing, and finish position.
- **Cloudtop Clash:** an original platform brawler against Zephyr. Move with Left/Right or A/D, press Up/W to jump and again to double-jump, Space sends a gust, and Down/S guards. Hits increase damage and knockback; leaving the arena costs a life. Last fighter with lives wins. Choose Finn, Ruby, Mina, or Bea. Both games have original artwork and characters, touch controls, pause/resume, automatic tab-away pause, and restart.

- **Desert Dino Dash:** an original dinosaur endless runner inspired by the browser offline game. Dot runs automatically; jump cacti with Space/Up/W or tap the picture, and hold Down/S or the touch Duck button for low-flying birds. Obstacles ramp up faster to a higher 720-pixel/second cap, birds arrive earlier, and cactus clusters and tighter obstacle spacing appear later; the scenery changes between day and evening, and the best score is stored on this device. One collision ends the run. P pauses; switching tabs also pauses.

- **Ruby’s Carrot Quest:** an original side-scrolling bunny platformer across six gardens, including new Bramble Bridge, Twilight Tunnels, and Stormlit Summit stages. Later gardens have wider gaps and more/faster snails. Hop on a snail to leave a stationary shell, then touch its side to kick it into other snails for points. Moving shells can hurt Ruby; hopping on one stops it. Carrots power Ruby up, improve jump height, and absorb one enemy hit. Collect sunseeds, hop onto snails from above, cross ponds, and reach each cottage. A midpoint flag saves the checkpoint for that garden. Left/Right or A/D moves; Space/Up/W jumps. Touch directions and Jump work together; P pauses. Three lives, visible garden-completion screens, and a final picnic victory after six gardens. Resting between gardens restores one life, up to three.

- **Garden Four:** four-in-a-row strategy against the computer. Easy chooses random moves, Medium wins or blocks immediate threats, and Hard searches ahead with alpha-beta minimax. Four across, down, or diagonally wins; a full board without a line is a draw.
- **Code Critter:** eight garden programming puzzles with a visual command builder, Forward/turn commands, repeat loops, and nested loops. Run or single-step a program, see the current block highlighted, collect every seed, and reach the flag. Programs are validated (no text evaluation), bounded to three loop nesting levels and 160 expanded steps. Hints, block removal, undo, reset, stop, and replay support debugging.

## Math corner · grades 1–5

Seven untimed games offer grade selection, ten-challenge rounds, visual models, optional hints, retry feedback, and a completion celebration. Wrong answers can be retried; no personal learning records are stored or sent to analytics. Grade labels describe practice levels rather than a specific school curriculum.

- **Number Garden:** addition/subtraction within 20, 100, 1,000, or 10,000 in grades 1–4, and decimals to hundredths in grade 5; includes word problems and seed counters for grade 1.
- **Sharing Picnic:** grade 1 equal groups and fair sharing, grade 2 groups of 2/5/10, grade 3 multiplication/division facts, grade 4 one-digit × two-digit, and grade 5 two-digit multiplication and exact division. Basket models support repeated addition and sharing.
- **Place Value Builders:** interactive digit boxes for tens/ones (grade 1), hundreds (grade 2), thousands (grade 3), hundred thousands (grade 4), and decimals through thousandths (grade 5), with expanded-form hints.
- **Fraction Bakery:** decorate halves/quarters in grade 1, more simple fractions in grade 2, models/equivalence in grade 3, models/equivalence/comparison in grade 4, and adding/subtracting unlike-denominator fractions in simplest form in grade 5. Equal-area tray models emphasize parts of the same whole. These early-grade fraction activities are introductory enrichment.

- **Pattern Path:** repeating shapes/counting (grade 1), skip-counting/backward sequences (grade 2), multiplication patterns (grade 3), alternating rules (grade 4), and decimal/growing-difference patterns (grade 5).
- **Chance Pond:** certain/possible/impossible (grade 1), likelihood categories (grade 2), probability fractions (grade 3), comparison of ponds (grade 4), and percentages/expected results with replacement (grade 5). Practice picks return the fish; expected counts are explicitly estimates, not guaranteed outcomes.
- **Mystery Number Workshop:** missing addends (grade 1), addition/subtraction unknowns (grade 2), missing factors/divisors (grade 3), two-step equations (grade 4), and simple parentheses/inverse operations (grade 5). Probability and algebra in early grades are introductory enrichment.

## Test

With Node.js 18 or newer, run `npm test` to check game rules. The site is static and can be deployed on any static host. Google Fonts is optional; system font fallbacks work offline. No accounts, API keys, or backend services are required to play. Optional Umami analytics needs a separately configured owner account.


## Optional shared analytics (Umami)

Umami is configured for the owner-provided website ID in `src/analytics-config.js`. Tracking is enabled on production visits after this configuration is deployed. Local browser counters cannot report visitors across different devices; this integration sends events to your Umami dashboard once configured.

1. In Umami Cloud or your existing self-hosted Umami installation, add the production website `https://dgrosher.github.io/AnnesGameWebsite/`.
2. Copy the public website ID and tracking script URL into `src/analytics-config.js`, and set `enabled: true`. These are public identifiers, not API secrets. Do not commit account passwords or API tokens.
3. Deploy the change, open the live website, launch a game, and check Umami’s realtime dashboard. Do not claim live collection until those visits appear.

The **Visitors** report estimates unique visitors; it cannot count identified people or reliably combine one person’s different devices. In **Pages**, filter paths under `/AnnesGameWebsite/games/` for each game’s visits and visitor estimates. The `game_open` event includes `game_id` and `game_name` for popularity reports. `game_restart` is separate so restarting a round does not create another game-open event. Opening a game means entering its screen; this does not measure time actively playing, wins, or completions. A visitor who opens several games remains one site visitor, so do not sum per-game visitors to count people.

The integration uses Umami’s cookie-free tracking; it does not add persistent player IDs or request player names or emails. Page URLs and referrers omit query strings and fragments. Localhost, Do Not Track, and Global Privacy Control visits are excluded. Ad blockers and disabled tracking can reduce counts. The site and all games work if analytics is unavailable. No analytics API credentials belong in the client code, and the private dashboard stays in your own Umami account.

### Diagnose missing visits

Umami works with GitHub Pages; it does not require a server in this repository. Open `analytics-check.html` on the live website and choose **Run analytics check**. This shows the deployed public website ID, reports Do Not Track / Global Privacy Control / Umami opt-out settings, tests the real tracking script, and observes the HTTP response for a single `analytics_check` event. It distinguishes a blocked script, a blocked collection request, a rejected website ID, and an account-disabled response. It never overrides privacy settings and never shows session/cache tokens. A successful response still needs confirmation in your own Umami Realtime dashboard. For missing accepted events, compare the displayed website ID with your selected Umami website, remove dashboard filters, and check traffic exclusions and account limits.
